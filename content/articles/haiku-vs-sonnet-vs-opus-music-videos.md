It's November 2026. AI can now make you a music video while you eat your lunch. So naturally I did the only reasonable thing: I made **three** of them, one with each Claude model, and then stared at the bill like it owed me money.

Same idea, same tool (Claude Code), same **medium effort** setting. The only thing I changed was the model: **Haiku 5.5**, **Sonnet** and **Opus 5.5**.

Here's what came back, what it cost, and whether the expensive one is worth it. (Spoiler: annoyingly, yes.)

## The bill

Every video is 30 seconds long. Claude Code estimates the cost from the tokens each model used. Here's the damage in rupees:

{{bars title="Cost per 30-second video" data="Haiku 5.5:5.83,Sonnet:28.59,Opus 5.5:92" prefix="₹" decimals="2" note="Estimated API cost reported by Claude Code. Medium effort for all three."}}

Opus cost **about 16 times** what Haiku did, and a bit over **3 times** Sonnet. For ₹92 you could also buy a very nice plate of momos. I'll let you decide which brings more joy.

But it's not only that Opus charges more per token. It also *thinks more*:

Opus used nearly **twice** as many tokens as Haiku. So you pay a higher rate, *and* you pay it more times. Double whammy.

## The real flex: Opus gave me a project, not just a video

Here's the part that surprised me most. Opus didn't just hand me a finished MP4. It built the whole thing as an **editable project** in HyperFrames, with separate scenes (planet, bed, hammock, party, sunset and night), a timeline and the song on its own track.

![The Opus music video opened as an editable HyperFrames project, with separate scenes on a timeline and the song on its own track](/articles/images/opus-hyperframes-project.webp "Opus's video as an editable HyperFrames project: every scene and the song, ready to tweak")

That means I can open it, swap a scene, retime the music or fix one frame myself, without asking the AI to regenerate everything. That's the difference between getting a **photo** of a cake and getting the **recipe**.

## So which model should you use?

- **Haiku: the intern who's surprisingly good.** Use it for first drafts, quick tests and anything you'll throw away. At ₹6 a go, you can afford ten tries.
- **Sonnet: the dependable teammate.** The best balance of quality and cost for most real work. This is the default I'd recommend.
- **Opus: the senior who costs senior money.** Use it when quality really matters, when the task is complex, or when you want a result you can build on. It's by far the best, and the bill reminds you of that.

My workflow now: **explore with Haiku, build with Sonnet, finish with Opus.** Cheap where it doesn't matter, expensive only where it does.

## The bigger picture

A year ago, "make me a music video" was a weekend project. Today it's a sentence and a cup of chai, and the *worst* of these three still produced something watchable for less than the price of a samosa.

The gap between the models is real, but so is the gap between all of them and what was possible not long ago. Whichever you pick, we're living in the future. It just sends you an invoice.
